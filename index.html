Theatre is a form of inquiry.

Essays that take a play as the occasion for thinking about what theatre is for, why we make it, and what it could become. Caught between the market on one side and the sermon on the other, theatre needs new ideas. The Review is where we start looking for them.

Read Issue One What we believe
What we believe

Answerable to neither the market nor the sermon.

Commercial theatre makes art answer to its investors. Didactic theatre makes it answer to a message. Either way the play becomes a means to something else. We want to recover what theatre can do when it answers only to the questions it raises.

  1. ITheatre is inquiry. It investigates what it is to be human, and does so in ways an argument cannot.
  2. IIA message is not a meaning. Art reduced to instruction fails as art, whatever it instructs.
  3. IIIWe are here to learn. We don’t arrive with a doctrine. We read widely, across centuries and cultures, as students rather than authorities, and we’re glad to be surprised.
  4. IVThe point is what comes next. We look back only to push forward, towards a new account of what theatre does, what works on stage, and why we keep making it at all.
  5. VPaid for by readers. No single patron. The Review answers to the people who read it.

How we write

The essay begins in the theatre and goes wherever the thinking goes.

We publish long-form criticism that thinks through plays: a theme raised by a production, followed through film, television, philosophy and history. Sometimes that means an old idea; more often it means a new question.

We treat controversies as cultural events to understand, not cases to prosecute. We criticise preachiness from any direction, and we commission writers from every tradition, including the ones we argue with.
